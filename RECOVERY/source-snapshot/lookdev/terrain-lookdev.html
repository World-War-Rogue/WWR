What the wipe is showing

Left — one filled square per plot

A faithful port of the live renderer. It samples terrainAt once at each plot's integer coordinate and paints a single fillRect. Shade is one bit — shade > 0.5 ? fill : alt — so every plot is one of two colours per biome, which is the checkerboard. Biome edges snap to plot corners because the biome is decided per plot and never blended.

Right — sampled per pixel, lit by a sun

The same noise functions, sampled in world space instead of once per plot, with biomes as blended weights rather than one enum. A height field carries relief shading from the upper left, which is what makes it read as land rather than colour. Salt cracks, wash channels and sandstone terraces fall out of that height field rather than being drawn on.

Worth checking at each stop

  • 14 — world. The view used to read the war. Today it is at its most grid-like; the proposal should read as regional country with no squares at all.
  • 42 — identity. Where bases stop being art. Terrain has to carry the map on its own just below this line.
  • 94 — home. Where the map opens and where a base should look placed into the ground rather than pasted onto it.
  • 190 — max. Close enough that material has to hold up: cracks, track ruts, debris.
Props here are placeholders, and deliberately so. The plan calls for a pre-rendered sprite atlas through the existing Blender pipeline, because procedurally-drawn canvas art was rejected twice on this project and trees and wrecks will fail the same way. What is drawn here is shape, scale, density and placement — enough to judge whether the composition works. Assume every prop is replaced by real art. The ground, by contrast, is the real technique at something close to final quality.

One difference from the shipping version: this page redraws the whole visible ground on every change. The real implementation caches ground into offscreen tiles keyed by zoom bucket, so it pays that cost once per pan rather than per frame. If a change feels briefly slow here, that is this page, not the plan.
Terrain is visual only — it never changes march paths, timing, combat, detection or objective eligibility. Deterministic from world, season and position: no rows, no bytes over the wire.
